When I started mixing raw food for my dog, it looked like science. It isn't. BARF (Biologically Appropriate Raw Food) is basically an attempt to feed your dog what it would eat in the wild — raw meat, bones, organs, plus a bit of what it would find along the way. No magic, just putting it together so the ration covers what its body needs.

## What goes in the bowl

The whole thing stands on four components:

- **Muscle meat** — the base of the bowl, most of the daily ration and the main protein source.
- **Raw meaty bones** — calcium and phosphorus for bones and teeth.
- **Organs** — mainly liver and kidneys. Small volume, loads of vitamins.
- **The rest** — vegetables, fruit, nuts. A supplement, not the base.

In the app that fourth component is simply one category called "other", so it doesn't get needlessly complicated. You don't need to figure out which box a carrot or a blueberry belongs to — you throw it in "other" and move on.

The ratio itself is mostly a matter of preference — most people go with around 70% muscle meat, 10% bones, 10% organs and 10% of the rest. What depends on your dog's weight, age and activity is the feeding coefficient — how much food it gets per day in total. You can calculate it properly with the [FEDIAF method](#pojem:fediaf) — or let the calculator in the app do it for you.

## You don't have to be scared of it

The most common worry I hear: "What if I mix it wrong?" Look — if you stick to ratios and don't feed your dog just one component, there isn't that much you can break. A bad combination means a bad ratio, not a bad ingredient.

And your dog will tell you anyway. Overdo the organs? Diarrhea. Overdo the bones? Rock-hard poop. Sounds gross, but it's actually an advantage — you get daily feedback on the lawn and adjust the ratios accordingly.

And even if your ratio is off for a while, in my opinion it still beats cheap kibble like pedigree or propesko. I'm not saying all kibble is bad — brit, for example, is mostly a solid brand, and dogs live perfectly fine on good kibble, it's a legit choice. But propesko is just garbage, and even an imperfectly mixed BARF beats it.

## Why bother

Better coat, calmer digestion, and you actually know what your dog ate. There's one catch: you have to plan. Weigh portions, watch ratios, know what's left in the fridge. That's exactly what annoyed me about raw feeding — and why the app exists.

> One note: I'm not a vet or a nutritionist. Before you start, talk to someone who does this professionally — especially with puppies, seniors or dogs with health issues.
